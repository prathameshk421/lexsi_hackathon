Frugal Analyst: a 3.35B model that answers database questions with few, verified tool calls

Team Dear_Basketball. Track 3, Agentic Fine-tuning. Problem statements 6 (Data and database agents) and 2 (Efficient trajectories). The work also touches 3 (Agentic distillation) and 9 (Reward design and reward-hacking defences).

Small models can call tools, but they waste calls and guess answers. We train Tiny Aya Global (CohereLabs/tiny-aya-global, 3.35B, LoRA r=16) to answer analytics questions over SQLite with two tools, get_schema and run_sql. The reward pays for a correct answer and for low cost: tool calls, rows returned and SQLite VM steps. An answer counts as correct only if a query that read a real table returned it, and the value is not a typed literal in that query's select list.

All data is synthetic and English: 5 databases and 48 question templates. One domain (saas, 100 test tasks) is held out from all training. In the 4 training domains, 12 of 38 templates are kept out of SFT. 38 of the 120 in-domain test tasks use them.

Pipeline. AgentTune does LoRA SFT on scripted oracle trajectories, then GRPO with real tool rollouts: 50 steps, then 150 more. AuditKit evaluates every model from saved traces. 95% intervals come from a paired bootstrap over tasks.

Results. On the held-out domain, task success goes from 0.080 zero-shot to 0.460 after SFT and 0.500 after 200 GRPO steps (+0.420, 95% CI +0.330 to +0.520). Mean latency falls from 11.6 s to 5.8 s. Tool-call name F1 rises from 0.677 to 0.925. In-domain, task success goes from 0.083 to 0.875.

GRPO on top of SFT has a mixed effect. Mean tool calls fall clearly: 2.34 to 2.23 in-domain (CI -0.192 to -0.033) and 2.44 to 2.27 held-out (CI -0.270 to -0.070). Accuracy does not change clearly: +0.067 in-domain (CI +0.000 to +0.142) and +0.040 held-out (CI +0.000 to +0.090). On the 38 in-domain tasks from templates that only GRPO trained on, success goes from 0.447 to 0.789. On the 82 tasks from templates that SFT saw, it falls from 0.976 to 0.915. After about 50 steps, most GRPO groups had no reward spread.

In a second experiment the teacher added one unneeded query on half the traces. SFT copied the habit. 30 GRPO steps cut mean tool calls from 2.89 to 2.80 (CI -0.150 to -0.033) at unchanged accuracy. The effect is small.

An offline audit on 40 validation tasks tests the evidence gate. Nine scripted cheats score 0.5 without it and 0.0 to 0.1 with it. Two gaps stay open: arithmetic on literals and an echoed filter value.

Negative results: the first GRPO run had no reward spread. Held-out code-lookup tasks stay hard (0.075). Limits: synthetic data, one seed, small test sets, no general-capability check. The 200-step model used a higher learning rate than the 50-step model. Compute: 20.3 GPU hours on Hugging Face Jobs, about 28.4 USD on two team accounts, failed runs included.
